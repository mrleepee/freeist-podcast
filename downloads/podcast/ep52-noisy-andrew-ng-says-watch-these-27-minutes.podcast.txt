AI is increasingly being described as a general-purpose technology, something on the scale of electricity in terms of the opportunities it creates for builders. The development cycle for AI applications has shortened dramatically. Where it once took six to twelve months to build an AI system, generative AI now enables the same work in around ten days. This shift is fundamentally changing how we approach invention. The new path to innovation is fast experimentation: build twenty prototypes, keep what works, and discard the rest. What's interesting is that the bottleneck has moved. Training a model used to be the limiting factor, but now the evaluations and testing phases are where development slows down.

The most exciting trend at present is agentic AI workflows. Traditional zero-shot prompting processes a request in a single pass, but agentic workflows use iterative loops. The system will outline a task, conduct research, draft a response, critique it, and then revise. This cycle can improve accuracy from around forty-eight percent to ninety-five percent on benchmark tests. Four major design patterns are emerging. Reflection allows the language model to critique and improve its own output. Tool use enables the model to decide when to search the web, execute code, or perform other actions. Planning lets it decompose complex tasks into sequences. And multi-agent collaboration has multiple AI agents with different roles working together to solve problems.

Visual AI represents another significant shift. Large multimodal agents can now process images and videos iteratively, enabling practical applications like counting objects, analysing video clips, and generating metadata. This opens considerable value from visual data that has been underutilised up to now.

A few additional trends merit attention. Hardware and software improvements are speeding up token generation. Models are increasingly being tuned specifically for agentic workflows and tool use. Data engineering is becoming more important as we grapple with managing unstructured text, images, and video. And the image processing revolution is just beginning.
