Last December, something quietly shifted in the world of programming. For the first time, A.I. coding tools started producing work so reliable that developers no longer needed to spend half their time correcting them. That single development has been quietly reshaping how many of us think about what it means to write software.

This episode explores the concept of vibe coding, the emergence of what some are calling Software Three point O, and why understanding these shifts matters more than ever for anyone building things with technology. The core insight here is about a new paradigm in software development. Software One point O was about writing explicit instructions line by line. Software Two point O came along when we started training neural networks with data instead. Now we appear to be entering Software Three point O, where programming happens through prompting. Context windows become the primary lever for steering large language models. Practical examples of this are already everywhere. Someone needed to install OpenClaw and simply copy-pasted the task to an A.I. agent. A restaurant owner wanted a menu redesigned, sent a photograph to an L.L.M., and received a perfectly rendered menu image back.

What makes certain tasks genuinely solvable by A.I. comes down to verifiability. When something can be automatically checked, like mathematics or code, A.I. tends to excel at it. This happens because the laboratories training these models use reinforcement learning with reward signals, which work best when you can easily determine whether something is correct. This explains what researchers call jagged intelligence. A.I. can be spectacularly capable in verifiable domains while remaining surprisingly limited in areas where verification is difficult or subjective.

For founders and builders, this creates a strategic implication. Targeting verifiable domains and investing in reinforcement learning environments for fine-tuning becomes a genuine competitive advantage. The next layer of this discussion distinguishes between vibe coding and agentic engineering. Vibe coding is about raising the floor for everyone, letting people who never considered themselves programmers build real things. Agentic engineering operates differently. It maintains quality standards while moving faster, coordinating multiple fallible but powerful agents to accomplish complex tasks.

What remains genuinely valuable for humans in this landscape surprises many people. Taste, judgment, and aesthetic sense cannot be outsourced. Understanding why something works at a fundamental level matters far more than memorizing A.P.I. details. Knowing the difference between tensor views and copies in your framework, even if you cannot recall the exact syntax, positions you as the director orchestrating A.I. agents rather than being directed by them.

A useful mental model emerging from recent thinking treats A.I. systems as summoned ghosts rather than thinking beings. They are shaped entirely by data and reward signals, not by evolution or intrinsic motivation. They lack the biological grounding that gives animals their coherent sense of self and purpose. This framing matters because it sets realistic expectations about what these systems can and cannot do independently.

The infrastructure of our digital world still assumes human operators. As agents become more capable, everything from how we design A.P.Is to how we structure permissions and communication protocols needs rebuilding from the ground up. Expect a future where agents represent people and organisations, negotiating and collaborating on our behalf across countless interactions we never need to see.

One principle cuts through all of this. You can outsource the mechanics of thinking, but genuine understanding remains firmly human. Deep comprehension of how and why systems work allows you to direct A.I. effectively, asking the right questions and evaluating responses critically. That human capacity for understanding is not going away, and it might be the most important skill worth cultivating right now.
