Andrew Ng stood on stage at YCombinator and said something most people missed: the next wave of software companies won't be SaaS. They'll be AI Native Agencies.

This isn't theoretical. Someone's already giving away the entire playbook for free, and it's built around a simple premise. Instead of selling monthly subscriptions to software, you sell outcomes. You use AI agents to deliver those outcomes at a fraction of the traditional cost, and you charge based on results.

The model works like this. Say a business needs customer support. A traditional SaaS company sells them a helpdesk tool for fifty dollars a seat per month. An AI Native Agency says: we'll handle your entire customer support operation using AI agents, and we'll charge you per ticket resolved. The business doesn't buy software. It buys solved problems.

Why is this happening now? Because large language models have crossed a threshold. They can handle complex, multi-step tasks that used to require a human sitting at a keyboard. Writing marketing copy, analysing data, qualifying leads, managing social media, processing invoices — these are all tasks that AI agents can now do reliably enough to charge real money for.

The playbook that's being shared for free lays out how to get started. Pick a repetitive, high-volume business process. Build an AI agent that can handle it end to end. Offer it as a service. Charge by the outcome, not by the hour or the seat. The target is ten thousand dollars per month in recurring revenue, and the claim is that it's achievable without writing much original code, because the intelligence comes from the language model, not from custom software.

The shift from SaaS to AI Agencies mirrors an earlier shift. In the early 2000s, companies moved from buying shrink-wrapped software to subscribing to cloud services. Now they're moving from subscribing to tools to subscribing to outcomes. The tool is invisible. The customer doesn't care how the work gets done. They just want it done.

This has implications for developers, too. The skills that matter are shifting. It's less about building CRUD applications and more about designing agent workflows, managing prompt chains, and orchestrating multiple AI systems to work together. The engineer of the next decade is less like a software developer and more like a process designer.

Andrew Ng's point at YC was that most people are still thinking about AI as a feature you add to existing software. That's the wrong framing. AI isn't a feature. It's a new kind of worker that never sleeps, never takes breaks, and works for the cost of compute. Build your business around that worker, and you've got an AI Native Agency.

The question isn't whether this model works. It's already working. The question is how fast it scales, and whether the ten thousand dollar per month target is conservative or ambitious. Given what AI agents can already do, it might be conservative.

The bottom line: if you're still thinking about building a SaaS, you might be building for the last era. The next one belongs to agencies that sell outcomes powered by AI.
