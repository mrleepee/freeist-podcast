I'm standing in the middle of Istanbul, that incredible city where East really does meet West, and I'm about to look at four different apartments—all of which could actually make you a Turkish citizen. Sounds a bit mental, doesn't it? But that's exactly what I did last week, and honestly, it's got me thinking about property investment in a whole new way.

Now, for those who don't know, Turkey has what's called a Citizenship by Investment programme. The minimum you need to put in is around $400,000, and once you've done that through property, you're eligible for a Turkish passport. It's been around for a while, but what's interesting is what's happening to the market right now.

My mate Keith, who's a proper Istanbul realtor, took me around the city centre to see what you actually get for your money. And let me tell you, the strategy you choose makes a massive difference. There's the one-shot approach—basically buying one property at the $400K-plus mark. That works, sure. But then there's what Keith calls the stacking approach, which is where things get really clever.

With stacking, you're buying multiple smaller properties, say two or three at around $125,000 to $250,000 each, and they add up to that $400K threshold. The beautiful thing about this? You've got way more negotiation power, and you're spreading your risk across different assets. More often than not, the returns are better too.

Alright, let's get into the properties. First up was this place on Hanam Fendi Street. We're talking about 150 square metres—quite spacious, three bedrooms, recently renovated. It's in downtown Istanbul, nestled between Osman Bay and Meia Koi. The interior is gorgeous, by the way. Arabesque style throughout, marble countertops, proper LG appliances, and three bathrooms. Three! The rental yield is sitting at around 5 percent gross, which translates to about $1,500 to $1,750 a month if it's furnished. And here's a lovely bonus—no service charges, because the owner actually owns the ground floor commercial space. So what do I think? Decent value for a downtown location. Much better than those tiny one-bedrooms you find further out at the same price.

Then we went to Devvon Bowmonti. Now this is where it gets fancy. Sixty-five square metres, one bedroom, but it's in a high-rise right in the heart of Bomti—which is basically prime downtown. The views are absolutely stunning. You can see the Bosphorus and the Golden Horn from the living room, and the building has all the bells and whistles: heated pool, gym, even a little market on-site. It's eight years old but immaculately maintained. The yield is more modest at 3 to 4 percent, but that's really a store of value play—you're buying into an amazing lifestyle and a premium location. Honestly, if I were choosing between this and a brand-new flat twenty minutes away, I'd pick this every single time.

Now property number three is where it gets interesting from an investment perspective. It's a duplex, about 100 square metres, and it's going for around $140,000. The building is ten to twelve years old, just a five-minute stroll from Bomti. But here's the thing—it needs a bit of work. We're talking maybe $10,000 to $15,000 to renovate the kitchen, bathrooms, and flooring. Once that's done, though, you're looking at a 6 to 7 percent gross yield, which comes down to about 5 percent after taxes. One crucial point: renovation costs do not count towards your $400,000 threshold for citizenship. So this is purely an investment play, not a lifestyle choice. If you want something to move into yourself, give this one a miss.

Finally, we checked out a budget option at around $115,000 to $117,000. Fifty square metres, one bedroom, in a relatively new building that's been freshly renovated. The yield is a solid 6 percent, and the beauty of this one is it's really easy to sell on. Perfect for stacking your way up to that $400K minimum.

Now, what's the market actually doing? Well, property prices have actually dropped by about 10 to 15 percent over the last year, which is great news if you're buying now. Meanwhile, rents are climbing. So yields are recovering, and that makes this a really favourable time to get involved.

A few things I took away from the tour. First, downtown really does offer better value than those developments way out in the suburbs. Second, don't dismiss secondary market properties—places that are eight to twelve years old can sometimes be far better investments than brand-new builds. Third, quality finishes matter enormously when it comes time to sell. And finally, if you're a foreign buyer, definitely avoid areas that are primarily designed for local Turkish tenants. The market dynamics are just different.
