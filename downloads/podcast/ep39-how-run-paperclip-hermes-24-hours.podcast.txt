In this episode you’ll learn three things: what Paperclip and Hermes actually do, how to get a basic multi‑agent system up and running in 24 hours, and the simple habits that turn a first‑day demo into a reliable workflow.

Imagine you’ve got a handful of AI agents each doing its own thing, and the whole setup feels more like a chaotic office than a productive team. That’s the problem Paperclip and Hermes solve together. Hermes is the worker – an AI that can use tools, run locally, and behave like an employee rather than a simple chatbot. Paperclip is the operating layer that gives that worker a place to belong, a set of roles, tickets, heartbeats and reporting structures. When you plug Hermes into Paperclip, you stop just chatting with AI and start building a tiny, manageable AI organisation.

The first 24 hours should be deliberately small. Don’t try to build a full‑blown AI company on day one. Instead, pick one clear business task – say, researching keywords, planning a blog post, drafting customer‑support replies, or setting up a basic SEO workflow. Create a handful of agent roles that match that task: a research agent, a content‑planning agent, an SEO agent, a support‑agent, and perhaps a progress‑monitor. Each role gets a narrow job description, a specific goal, limits on what it can do, and a preferred output style. The research agent shouldn’t be writing the final article, and the support agent shouldn’t be juggling SEO strategy. Keeping duties distinct is what makes the workflow feel like a real company dashboard rather than a mess of overlapping chat.

Paperclip turns these jobs into tickets you can see, track and update. You’ll watch agents post updates, move tasks from “in progress” to “done”, and report back on what they’ve accomplished. That visibility is the key advantage over a single‑assistant setup. Once the first workflow runs, review the output, note where the agents stumbled, and refine their instructions. Tighten the roles, remove any agent that isn’t pulling its weight, and simplify tasks that proved too complex. This iterative tweaking is how you move from a demo to a system that actually scales.

The smartest move after those first 24 hours is to stay disciplined about growth. Add complexity only when the workflow genuinely needs it. Start with one clean workflow, prove it works, then expand the team or add new roles. Scaling chaos is the fastest way to break a multi‑agent system, while building incrementally turns a handful of useful bots into a reliable AI workforce.

So today we learned that Hermes provides the worker, Paperclip provides the operating platform, and the key to a successful first day is a single focused goal, clear role assignments, and visible ticket management. By keeping the setup simple and refining iteratively, you can transform a noisy swarm of agents into a tidy team that you can actually manage.
