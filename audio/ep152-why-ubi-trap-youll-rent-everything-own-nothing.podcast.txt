Simon Dixon is an angel investor, an early Bitcoin investor, and a former investment banker. He argues that Universal Basic Income is not a path to prosperity. It is a mechanism for a massive hidden wealth transfer.

Dixon's thesis is straightforward. Artificial intelligence and new financial technologies will make daily consumption cheaper. Simultaneously, they will make actual ownership prohibitively expensive. Universal Basic Income is framed as mere consumption support. It gives you just enough to keep buying basic goods, but it creates no wealth. You get a monthly allowance to rent your life from the people who actually own the assets.

Here is the catch. Artificial intelligence and asset tokenisation are accelerating this divide. Financial institutions want to hold the physical, productive assets. Retail investors are left holding digital paper contracts. Daily life gets cheaper, but acquiring hard property becomes structurally impossible for the average person. Historically, the middle class was built on property and business ownership. The new system threatens to permanently separate the act of consuming things from the act of owning them.

People love to cite the phrase you will own nothing and be happy as evidence of a plotted agenda. That is a misframed conspiracy. The phrase actually originated from a twenty sixteen World Economic Forum prediction about the sharing economy. It was a descriptive forecast, not a secret plot. Furthermore, the idea that universal income will automatically concentrate wealth is Dixon's opinion, not settled economics. There are no shadowy manufactured crises designed to strip your wealth. There is only a structural shift in technology and finance.

But you do need to follow the money. Look at massive consumer-dependent retailers like Walmart. A corporate behemoth that relies entirely on aggregate consumer demand has a clear structural incentive to back a universal income. If automation eventually deletes the working-class paycheque, nobody has the capital to buy the goods. A guaranteed government stipend simply keeps the consumer engine running. Walmart has never officially endorsed this policy, but the financial alignment is obvious. The system needs consumers, not owners.

Dixon warns of a subscription industrial complex where citizens rent access to everything. A government cheque will never equate to holding productive assets. To survive this shift, he states that individuals need a five to ten year financial plan. He recommends maximizing your personal productivity, spending less than you earn, and using the difference to acquire hard assets. Specifically, he recommends buying Bitcoin and diversifying your holdings. That is his stated strategy, not neutral advice, and certainly not financial advice.

Your sovereignty depends entirely on what you control. If your entire existence is funded by a state-issued digital allowance, your consent means nothing. Build your own structure before one is built around you.
