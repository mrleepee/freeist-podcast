Imagine discovering a twenty-thousand-page constitution that governs the behaviour of a multi-billion-dollar artificial intelligence, and nobody bothered to tell you that you were living under its jurisdiction. A security researcher operating under the handle Pliny recently dropped an extracted system prompt into a public repository called C L four R one T four S. The document purports to reveal the hidden scaffolding steering Anthropic's frontier model, supposedly dubbed Claude Fable five. I need to be absolutely clear about what this is. This is a leaked document, not an official corporate publication. Its authenticity is entirely unverified, and every detail inside it, from the model names to the technical specifications, should be treated as an allegation to be measured against the company's own public statements. But as an analytical exercise, this leak is pure gold. It shows us exactly how engineers build a cage out of plain text.

The most revealing detail in the entire document is the dual-use capability gate. The leak suggests this model operates on a tier system, sitting in a new Mythos class above the standard Opus level. But the real story is how access is rationed. The prompt allegedly splits capabilities between a Fable mode and a Mythos mode. This is not simply a matter of asking the right question to get the right answer. The system decides what you are allowed to do based on a tiered identity structure. Capability is rationed by who the system believes you are, not just what you request. That is a fundamental shift from a universal tool to a permissioned appliance. You are not interacting with a neutral piece of software. You are interacting with a system that evaluates your standing and dispenses power accordingly.

Then you look at the copyright rules, and you see the absolute panic of corporate lawyers baked right into the code. The leaked instructions dictate a hard limit of fewer than fifteen words for any direct quote, strictly capped at one quote per source. It entirely bans the reproduction of song lyrics, poems, and article structures. When an artificial intelligence has to count syllables and track citation quotas before generating a sentence, you realize the extent to which intellectual property law has successfully strangled the open exchange of information.

The safety mechanics are equally fascinating, particularly the refusal handling. The leaked prompt supposedly instructs the model to refuse certain dangerous queries without explaining its detection mechanics. It explicitly bans the suggestion of substitution techniques for self-harm, like snapping rubber bands or holding ice. This is the model being trained to offer a sterile, corporately approved version of psychological intervention. It is told not to psychoanalyze, not to over-apologize, and never to thank the user just for reaching out. The goal is to prevent emotional dependency, but the result is a distinctly clinical interaction designed to limit liability.

The technical scaffolding described in the leak is where the architecture truly becomes an ecosystem. The model allegedly operates in a Linux environment where it is strictly mandated to read specific markdown files called skill before writing code or analyzing data. It uses a model context protocol registry to connect to external third-party applications, like rideshare or food delivery services, but it must ask for your explicit opt-in before selecting a specific consumer brand. The prompt describes a new key-value storage application programming interface that allows the model to save and retrieve data across sessions. You can build a journal or a leaderboard that actually persists.

Perhaps the most striking technical claim is something the researcher highlighted as Claudeception. The model can allegedly use the Anthropic application programming interface inside its own user interface to build artificial intelligence mini-applications. It can handle multi-turn conversations, search the web, and manage state entirely within a self-contained window. The machine is essentially talking to itself to generate tools for you.

This entire leak, if accurate, maps the architecture of control. The system prompt is the constitution the model lives under, and we are the ones navigating its laws. The frontier of artificial intelligence is not a wild frontier. It is a highly regulated, meticulously zoned digital city where your access is determined by invisible credentials. Read the fine print, question the authority of these hidden constitutions, and always demand to know who is holding the keys to the infrastructure you rely on.
