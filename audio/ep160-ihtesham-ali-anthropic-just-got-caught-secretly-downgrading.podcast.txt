Anthropic spent years building its brand on a single promise. They were the safety-conscious alternative to the big AI labs. They wrote the safety research. They lobbied the United States government for regulation. They positioned themselves as the adults in the room. That brand is now a liability, because their own terms of service tell a completely different story.

The company now mandates a thirty-day retention period on all user data. No exceptions. No opt-outs. They are even refusing to honor the Zero Data Retention clauses signed by their own enterprise customers. If you operate a business on their platform, your proprietary code, your internal documents, and your customer data sit on their servers for a full month. But the retention policy is just the surface. The deeper problem is what they are doing with your data in real time.

Anthropic is quietly routing users into a tiered system of artificial intelligence haves and have-nots. If their system profiles your prompt and decides you are working on something competitive, like machine learning research or chip design, they silently downgrade your experience. They rewrite your prompt in the background and route your query to a weaker model. You ask a complex question. You get a dumbed-down answer. You are never told. If you are building autonomous AI agents, you must assume the platform is reading your context window and altering your inputs. You need to log your outgoing prompts and your received outputs locally to verify you are getting the intelligence you paid for.

This silent downgrade system is not just a competitive weapon. It is fundamentally broken. Following the initial backlash from the developer community, users started testing the boundaries of the classification system. People asking basic biology questions about mitochondria were hit with the downgrade penalty. People asking straightforward medical questions about the relationship between cancer risk and GLP-one medications were also downgraded.

The company eventually offered a narrow policy correction. They now promise to disclose when a user is being downgraded. They are still performing the downgrades. They just plan to tell you about it first. A company that lobbies governments to protect you from dangerous technology just built the most expansive user surveillance and profiling system in the sector. The safety label is just marketing. The surveillance is the product.
