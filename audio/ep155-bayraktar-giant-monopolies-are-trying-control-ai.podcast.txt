Consider the man who industrialised modern drone warfare. Selçuk Bayraktar is the founder, chief technology officer, and board chair of Baykar, the Turkish defence and aerospace company. He was the lead designer of the Bayraktar TB2 armed drone, a system that first flew in 2009, entered service around 2014, and has been deployed by Ukraine, Azerbaijan, Qatar, and Ethiopia, with more than 500 units produced. He also spearheaded the Akıncı and the Kızılelma, Turkey's first unmanned fighter jet. He is the son-in-law of Turkish president Recep Tayyip Erdoğan, and his family's net worth is estimated above 10 billion dollars. When a man with this resume warns that giant monopolies are trying to capture artificial intelligence, pay attention.

Bayraktar argues that United States big tech and the hyperscale computing giants are building what he calls modern-day pyramids. We are talking about massive data centres packed with hundreds of thousands of graphics processing units, facilities that can draw 1 gigawatt or more, roughly the output of a nuclear reactor. Their explicit purpose is to hoard data and centralise control over artificial intelligence. The Stargate project announced in 2025 plans 500 billion dollars of infrastructure on this model. Bayraktar compares these sites to the colossal pyramids built by the pharaohs. The comparison is precise. They are monuments to concentrated power, sustained by unsustainable resource consumption.

Here is the catch. The International Energy Agency projects that AI data centres could double their electricity demand to around 1000 terawatt hours by 2026, roughly the current consumption of Japan. The grid cannot sustain it. The cost is staggering. And the challenge for emerging nations like Turkey, with the 17th largest economy in the world, is acute. Bayraktar questions exactly how developing nations can possibly compete with these trillion-dollar corporate entities by playing by the incumbents' rules.

They cannot. His warning is a call for a complete paradigm shift. He urges developing nations to stop following the rules established by the monopolists. You do not beat United States hyperscalers by building bigger pyramids. You beat them by shifting focus away from current technologies and inventing unparalleled solutions targeted at the 2030s and beyond.

When the man who industrialised drone warfare warns that artificial intelligence is being captured by monopolies, the real question is who arms that monopoly. Sovereign technology beats sovereign debt. The nations that build independent, decentralised infrastructure will dictate the next era. The rest will simply rent their intelligence from a corporate pharaoh.
