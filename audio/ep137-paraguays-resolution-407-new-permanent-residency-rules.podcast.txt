Spent three years building your career, paid for an MBA, and used it to secure permanent residency in Paraguay? Congratulations — that piece of paper is now worthless. On the twenty eighth of May, Paraguay's migration authority published Resolution four hundred and seven, and it quietly eliminated one of the most popular routes to permanent residency in South America.

If you have been watching the expat forums, you probably already know something changed. The announcement caught most of the relocation community off guard — not because the government announced it with fanfare, but because they did not announce it at all. One day the rules were one thing; the next day they were something else. That is increasingly how Paraguay does policy.

The good news is that the changes are now consolidated in one place. Previously, the requirements for upgrading from temporary to permanent residency were scattered across six separate regulations, some contradicting each other, all requiring careful archaeology to piece together. Resolution four hundred and seven sweeps those aside and replaces them with a single, unified framework. It is simpler, it is at least less likely to keep shifting under your feet, and that alone counts as genuine progress.

The core test is now solvency. The authorities want to see that you can support yourself in the country — that you have actual money coming in, not just a name on a form. The university degree pathway is gone. The five thousand dollar bank deposit is gone. What remains is real economic activity: income from employment, a pension, investments you live off, or consulting work you do remotely. The message is clear. Do not park a lump sum and call it proof of life. Show us the cash flow.

Everything now runs on sworn declarations, which means the stakes are higher. If you lie on the form, you can face criminal liability. The days of declaring zero income every month and hoping no one noticed are over — at least as a long term strategy.

Here is what matters immediately. Temporary residency is not affected. If you are applying for temporary residency right now, the process has not changed for you. The permanent residency finish line is what moved.

Why upgrade to permanent at all? You could simply keep renewing temporary residency every two years. It is not the end of the world, but there are two real problems. First, cost in time and money. Each renewal restarts the whole process, either your own hours spent on paperwork or money paid to someone to handle it for you. Permanent residency renews every ten years instead. Over a decade, that difference compounds into something meaningful.

Second, and more importantly, protection from future rule changes. The authorities keep rewriting the playbook. It is entirely plausible that in a couple of years they introduce income proof requirements for temporary residency, or raise the fees substantially, or restrict the categories further. Permanent residency, once you hold it and keep it in good standing, is theoretically insulated from those changes. That insulation is the main reason to upgrade while you can.

The practical question then becomes how to qualify. There are twelve categories in total, but roughly ninety percent of applicants fit one of two: independent worker or rentista, which is the term for someone living off income from abroad who does not pay tax inside Paraguay.

Contrary to what many assume, you can still qualify under the rentista route even without paying taxes in Paraguay — though it is more demanding. You need to prove you receive money from outside the country, typically showing three things: a contract with your employer or clients, the payment channel through which you are actually paid, and any foreign documents translated into Spanish and apostilled or legalized where required. That last part costs money and the authorities can ask plenty of questions, but it is workable even if you have been filing at zero up to now. Platform work through services like Upwork will likely count too, though that is brand new territory and remains untested in practice.

If you are more than three months away from your conversion date, the cleaner option is to pay some tax in Paraguay, and you only need to do it for the three months before your upgrade. Declare a modest salary of around six hundred dollars per month, which costs roughly sixty dollars in tax per month — so under two hundred dollars total for the quarter.

This route beats leaning on foreign contracts for several reasons. The tax filing comes from Paraguay's own government, so the authorities cannot challenge their own records. It demonstrates local roots. The upgrade is ultimately decided case by case, and clean local proof makes a yes more likely. You stay in control because your proof is filed through the system and is entirely your own — you are not waiting on an employer to write you a letter or a bank to produce a statement.

The standing advice from people who do this professionally is to always pay a little local tax as insurance, precisely so a change like Resolution four hundred and seven does not catch you out. Many advisors told their clients to pay nothing and keep their options open. Those clients are now scrambling.

The other categories cover shareholders, pensioners, property owners, employees, and professionals, among others. For pensioners, a certificate showing the amount and payment channels is sufficient. For property owners, rental income can count as proof — but only if you have owned the property for more than three years. A unit bought last year does not qualify. That three-year holding period is strict.

If you have no income at all, perhaps you are living off cryptocurrency after a good run, you can still issue consulting invoices and pay a small amount of local tax to establish solvency. That is honest, it works, and it is simpler than fabricating something.

The action plan is straightforward. Identify which of the twelve categories fits your situation — most likely independent worker or rentista. Gather proof of how your money moves and who pays you. Translate the foreign documents into Spanish. Do not lie on the sworn declaration. That last point bears repeating. The criminal liability is real, and it is not worth the risk.

One thread runs through every category: show where the money comes from and where it goes. That is the test now. Paraguay has decided it wants residents who are genuinely present, not paper participants. Whether you agree with that direction or not, the path forward is to work within it.

If you are already on temporary residency, the window for upgrading under the current rules is open. The rules may change again. The cost of paying a small amount of local tax for a few months is modest compared to the cost of being caught out later. Take the step, document carefully, and secure the position while you can.
