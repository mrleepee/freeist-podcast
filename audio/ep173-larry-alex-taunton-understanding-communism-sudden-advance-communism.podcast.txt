In our continuing look at how ideologies compete for control over your life, we covered the anarchist who tried to destroy Karl Marx's movement. Now we take the next step. How did Marx's ideas survive their own economic failures? They quietly changed tactics.

Karl Marx believed impersonal economic forces would inevitably drive the working class to violently overthrow the ruling class. That revolution never happened in the West. Democratic reforms gave workers a voice. A robust, Bible-centred Christian culture gave them hope and purpose. People who have hope and a stake in their own future do not burn their cities down. When the violent takeover failed, the ideology adapted. An Italian Marxist named Antonio Gramsci realised that Western institutions like the family, education, and religion were simply too strong for a direct assault. He proposed a Trojan Horse strategy. Rather than meeting the state's monopoly on force head-on, this new approach quietly infiltrates and undermines society's cultural pillars from within to cause a collapse.

The modern tools of this strategy are critical race theory and intersectionality. These frameworks expand the concept of the oppressed far beyond the working class. They divide society into rigid identity groups based on race, gender, sexuality, and religion. The goal is to assign fictional guilt to specific groups to justify the seizure of power and assets under the guise of social justice. It is a brilliant manipulation of human nature. Marxism cynically exploits human greed by promising you what belongs to someone else. It feeds the desire to control others through tyrannical social enforcement. It gives you a convenient excuse for your personal failures by blaming them on the perceived treachery of your neighbours.

We have seen the endgame of this manipulation. Marxist ideologies have historically led to totalitarianism and the deaths of over one hundred twenty-five million people in the twentieth century across dozens of nations. The state is a service provider with a monopoly on force. Ideologies like this simply justify expanding that force indefinitely. The historical defence against this power grab has been a large Christian population. Christians tend to recognise Marxism's inherent godlessness immediately. Marxists view Christianity as the foundational block holding up Western culture. Pull that block out, and the free world collapses.

Consent is not implied. Assigning original sin to an entire demographic to justify seizing their property is the exact opposite of voluntary exchange. The question is whether you can recognise a coerced compliance structure when it dresses itself up as social justice.
