The Titanic got six warnings about icebergs the night it sank. It steamed full ahead anyway. The captain assumed the ship was unsinkable. Pavel Durov, the founder of Telegram, draws exactly this parallel when he looks at the current state of personal freedom. Western governments are steering directly into the iceberg of mass surveillance, convinced their democratic institutions are too strong to sink. They are adopting the censorship and monitoring tactics you used to find only in Russia, China, and Iran. The justification is always emotional. Protect the children. Fight crime. Stop hate. But when you follow the policy trail, the target is never just criminals. The target is public discourse itself.

People in Western democracies are now facing arrests and fines for political speech and insults posted online. Look at the United Kingdom. Look at Germany. When the state starts prosecuting citizens for hurt feelings, self-censorship follows. Officials are pushing hard for mandatory identity verification for social media use. Once your digital identity is tied to your physical passport, your right to dissent evaporates.

Consider the proposals to ban social media for teenagers under sixteen. They are gaining traction in the European Union, the United Kingdom, and Australia. These bans will fail. We know this because Russia and Iran already tried it. Users just bypass the blocks with virtual private networks. Here is the catch. When you force teenagers onto these networks to access basic communication, you expose them to highly illegal and dangerous content that mainstream platforms actively block. The cure is worse than the disease.

Protecting children is the ultimate Trojan horse. It bypasses logic. The United Kingdom Online Safety Act and the European Union chat control initiative are sold as child protection measures. Government officials privately admit these laws are designed to mandate mass surveillance and control public conversation. They want backdoor access to your private messages. But weakening encryption only harms law-abiding citizens. Real criminals will just build their own secure applications or route their traffic through hidden servers. Government databases are notoriously vulnerable to corrupt officials. Handing the state a master key to your private messages guarantees data leaks, blackmail, and physical crimes like kidnapping.

Authoritarian governments intentionally create contradictory and overly complex regulations to make compliance impossible. This lets them criminalize any business, then selectively prosecute only those who refuse to fall in line with political demands. The European Union and France are using this exact tactic against technology platforms right now. They demand the suppression of inconvenient political narratives. Debates around the origins of coronavirus and government mandates were censored as dangerous conspiracy theories, only to be proven entirely worthy of public discussion later.

Artificial intelligence makes this uniquely dangerous. The Soviet KGB was limited by human resources. They needed actual people to tail suspects, read mail, and wiretap phones. Modern AI lets governments automatically monitor, analyze, and map every private message and personal relationship without human limitation. There is no backup Western civilization to escape to. You cannot just pack your bags and move to the free world, because the free world is busy building its own digital cage. Sovereignty requires recognizing the gravity of this situation and refusing to comply with systems designed to control your speech.
